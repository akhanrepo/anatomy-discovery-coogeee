First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **c1** established: Fabricius's De venarum ostiolis (1603) is the earliest known detailed description of the valves of the veins, though earlier anatomists, including Canano and Amato Lusitano, had observed them.
- **c2** established: Fabricius believed the valves slowed the flow of blood away from the heart so that it would not rush into the hands and feet.
- **c3** established: His student William Harvey reinterpreted the valves as preventing backflow towards the extremities, evidence that venous blood flows towards the heart.
