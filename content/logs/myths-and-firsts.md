First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **t1** contested · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): Isaac Vossius, in a book published in London in 1685, was among the first Europeans to claim that the Chinese had known the circulation of the blood; Paul Unschuld translates the relevant passages of the Huangdi Neijing as describing neither blood circulation nor a pumping heart.
- **t2** established: In drawings of about 1512–13, Leonardo proposed that eddies forming in the sinuses behind the aortic valve cusps help close the valve, a mechanism later confirmed by modern studies including MRI; his anatomical notes went unpublished for centuries.
- **t3** established: Michael Servetus described the pulmonary circulation in a suppressed theological book of 1553; Valverde put it in an anatomy book in 1556, crediting Colombo, whose own book appeared in 1559.
- **t4** established: In August 1677 the medical student Johan Ham showed Antonie van Leeuwenhoek moving 'animalcules' in semen; Leeuwenhoek reported them to the Royal Society that November and recorded that Ham had seen them first.
- **t5** established: Paolo Mascagni described lymphatics in the cerebral dura mater in his 1787 atlas; two independent groups reported meningeal lymphatic vessels again in 2015.
- **t6** contested: In 2018 Benias, Theise and colleagues reported fluid-filled spaces in connective tissue throughout the body; press coverage called it a 'new organ', a label other anatomists disputed.
