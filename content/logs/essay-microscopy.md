First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **e1** established: In two letters printed at Bologna in 1661, Malpighi used a microscope to show that the lung is made of tiny air sacs and, working on frogs with Carlo Fracassati, observed blood moving through minute vessels between arteries and veins.
- **e2** established: In August 1677 the medical student Johan Ham showed Antonie van Leeuwenhoek moving 'animalcules' in semen; Leeuwenhoek reported them to the Royal Society that November and recorded that Ham had seen them first.
- **e3** established: In his Traité des membranes (1800), Xavier Bichat argued that organs are built from a limited set of tissues, distinguished 21 kinds, and worked without a microscope.
- **e4** contested: Schleiden (for plants, 1838) and Schwann (for animals, 1839) proposed that living things are built from cells; Schwann thought new cells formed out of a formless fluid, a view later overturned by Remak's work on cell division and by Virchow's principle that every cell comes from a cell.
- **e5** established: In 1869 Paul Langerhans described distinct clusters of cells in the pancreas, later named the islets of Langerhans, whose role in producing insulin was established only in the twentieth century.
- **e6** established: At Toronto in 1921–22, Banting and Best, in Macleod's department, prepared a pancreatic extract that lowered blood sugar, and Collip purified it for human use.
- **e7** established: Cajal used an improved version of Golgi's stain, which blackens a few whole nerve cells, to show in 1888–89 that nerve cells are separate units.
- **e8** established: On 22 May 1954 Nature published two independent papers, by A. F. Huxley and Niedergerke and by H. E. Huxley and Hanson, showing that muscle filaments keep their length and slide past each other during contraction.
