First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **c1** established: Galen described the nerve that descends from the brain, loops back up and reaches the larynx, and demonstrated before an audience in Rome that cutting it silenced a squealing pig.
- **c2** established: Vesalius later repeated Galen's pig demonstration, tying and then releasing the recurrent laryngeal nerves.
- **c3** contested: Ancient and modern retellings differ on which nerves were tied in some of Galen's voice demonstrations; one account describes the intercostal nerves.
