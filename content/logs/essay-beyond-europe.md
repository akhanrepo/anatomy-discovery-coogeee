First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **e1** established: The Edwin Smith papyrus is a copy made around 1700–1600 BCE from older material; it contains the earliest known written descriptions of the brain's surface, the meninges and the cerebrospinal fluid.
- **e2** established: Its cases give each injury a prognosis, and some are judged untreatable.
- **e3** contested: The Suśrutasaṃhitā describes preparing a body for study by leaving it in water for several days and then removing its layers one by one.
- **e4** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): Specialist scholarship treats the Suśrutasaṃhitā as a layered text whose composition may have begun in the last centuries BCE and was completed later, not as a 6th-century BCE work.
- **e5** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): In the Huangdi Neijing the vessels carry qi and only sometimes blood, and specialist translators read it as describing neither blood circulation nor a pumping heart.
- **e6** established: From 1026, by imperial edict, Wang Weiyi oversaw the making of bronze acupuncture figures and an accompanying book, completed around 1027, which served as a national standard for channels and points.
- **e7** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): Ibn al-Haytham wrote the Book of Optics between about 1011 and 1021; he argued for intromission, in which light from every point of an object enters the eye, and his account of the eye drew on Galenic anatomy.
- **e8** established: Ibn al-Nafis, in his commentary on the anatomy of Ibn Sina's Canon, rejected the idea that blood passes through pores in the septum and described it travelling through the lungs; the manuscript was rediscovered in Berlin in 1924 by the Egyptian physician Muhyo al-Deen Altatawi.
- **e9** contested: Whether Ibn al-Nafis's description reached sixteenth-century European anatomists is debated; no documentary chain has been established.
- **e10** established: Mansur ibn Ilyas's anatomical treatise, dedicated to Pir Muhammad Bahadur, ruler of Fars, is the first known Islamic anatomical text with full-body illustrations, with five chapters on bones, nerves, muscles, veins and arteries.
- **e11** established: The Wellcome Collection's Ayurvedic Man, an 18th-century painting, was probably made by an Ayurvedic physician, Tibetan artists and a calligrapher in Kathmandu; it shows the organs as receptacles for the bodily fluids, captioned with extracts from the Bhāvaprakāśa.
- **e12** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine, written in the early eleventh century, organised Greek medicine, including Galen's anatomy, into a systematic textbook.
- **e13** contested: The Canon was translated into Latin in the twelfth century, traditionally credited to Gerard of Cremona, and became a standard textbook in European medical schools for centuries.
