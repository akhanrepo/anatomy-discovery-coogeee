First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **c1** established: Aselli saw the lacteals in 1622 while vivisecting a dog that had been fed shortly before.
- **c2** established: His account, De lactibus sive lacteis venis, was published after his death, in Milan in 1627, with colour-printed woodcuts.
- **c3** established: Aselli believed the lacteals carried chyle to the liver; Jean Pecquet's discovery of the thoracic duct around 1650–51 showed where they really lead.
