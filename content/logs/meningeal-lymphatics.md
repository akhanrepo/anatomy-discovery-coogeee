First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **c1** established: In 2015 two independent groups, Louveau and colleagues in Nature and Aspelund and colleagues in the Journal of Experimental Medicine, reported lymphatic vessels in the meninges.
- **c2** established: Paolo Mascagni had described lymphatics in the cerebral dura mater in his 1787 atlas of the lymphatic system, written in Latin and largely overlooked.
- **c3** contested: Later nineteenth-century work, notably by Key and Retzius, influenced the long-standing view that the brain lacks lymphatic drainage.
