First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **t1** established: The Edwin Smith Papyrus, a copy made around 1700–1600 BCE from older material, contains the earliest known written descriptions of the brain's surface, the meninges and the cerebrospinal fluid.
- **t2** established: Alcmaeon identified the brain as the seat of understanding and distinguished understanding from perception.
- **t3** established: Herophilus distinguished nerves from blood vessels and tendons, separated sensory from motor nerves, and described several pairs of cranial nerves.
- **t4** established: Galen demonstrated before an audience in Rome that cutting the recurrent laryngeal nerves silenced a squealing pig.
- **t5** established: Thomas Willis's Cerebri anatome (1664) gave the first complete description of the ring of arteries at the base of the brain.
- **t6** established: Using a modified version of Golgi's stain, Cajal showed in 1888–89 that nerve cells are separate units rather than a continuous network.
