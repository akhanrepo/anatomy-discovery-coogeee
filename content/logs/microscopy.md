First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **t1** established: Harvey inferred but never saw the connection between arteries and veins; in 1661 Malpighi, with Carlo Fracassati, observed blood in minute vessels of the frog lung.
- **t2** established: In August 1677 the medical student Johan Ham showed Antonie van Leeuwenhoek moving 'animalcules' in semen; Leeuwenhoek reported them to the Royal Society that November and recorded that Ham had seen them first.
- **t3** established: In his Traité des membranes (1800), Xavier Bichat argued that organs are built from a limited set of tissues, distinguished 21 kinds, and worked without a microscope.
- **t4** established: In 1869 Paul Langerhans described distinct clusters of cells in the pancreas, later named the islets of Langerhans, whose role in producing insulin was established only in the twentieth century.
- **t5** established: Cajal used an improved version of Golgi's stain, which blackens a few whole nerve cells, to show in 1888–89 that nerve cells are separate units.
