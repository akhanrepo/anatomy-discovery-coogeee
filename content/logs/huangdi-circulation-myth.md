First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **c1** established: Isaac Vossius, in a book published in London in 1685, was among the first Europeans to claim that the Chinese had known the circulation of the blood for thousands of years, citing texts including the Huangdi Neijing.
- **c2** contested · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): Joseph Needham's translations suggested the classics described a circulation of vital energy with the heart as a pump; Paul Unschuld translates the same passages as describing neither blood circulation nor a pumping heart.
- **c3** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): In the Huangdi Neijing the vessels carry qi, and only sometimes blood.
