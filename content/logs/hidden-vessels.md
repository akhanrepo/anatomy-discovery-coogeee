First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **t1** established: Aselli saw the lacteals in 1622 while vivisecting a dog that had been fed shortly before, and believed they carried chyle to the liver.
- **t2** established: Jean Pecquet's discovery of the thoracic duct, published in his Experimenta nova anatomica (1651), showed that the lacteals do not lead to the liver.
- **t3** established: Paolo Mascagni's atlas of the lymphatic system, published in 1787, described lymphatics in the cerebral dura mater; it was written in Latin and largely overlooked.
- **t4** contested: Later nineteenth-century work, notably by Key and Retzius, influenced the long-standing view that the brain lacks lymphatic drainage.
- **t5** established: In 2015 two independent groups, Louveau and colleagues in Nature and Aspelund and colleagues in the Journal of Experimental Medicine, reported lymphatic vessels in the meninges.
