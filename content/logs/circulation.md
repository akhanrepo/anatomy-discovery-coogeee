First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **t1** established: In Galenic physiology blood was made continuously in the liver from digested food, carried outwards by the veins and consumed by the body; Galen taught that blood passes from the right to the left side of the heart through pores in the septum, and Ibn Sina's Canon repeated the teaching.
- **t2** established: Ibn al-Nafis rejected the septal pores and described blood passing from the right ventricle through the lungs to the left.
- **t3** contested: In Europe the lung route was described by Servetus in a suppressed theological book of 1553, by Valverde in print in 1556, and by Colombo in 1559.
- **t4** established: Fabricius described the valves of the veins in 1603 but believed they slowed blood flowing away from the heart; his student William Harvey reinterpreted them as evidence that venous blood flows towards the heart, and argued in De motu cordis (1628) that the blood circulates.
- **t5** established: Harvey inferred but never saw the connection between arteries and veins; in 1661 Malpighi, with Carlo Fracassati, observed blood in minute vessels of the frog lung.
