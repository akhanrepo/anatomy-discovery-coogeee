First-pass verification from the October 2026 accuracy review (docs/research/accuracy-review-2026-10.md), then claim-by-claim against the sources listed.

- **t1** established: The Edwin Smith Papyrus, a copy made around 1700–1600 BCE from older material, contains the earliest known written descriptions of the brain's surface, the meninges and the cerebrospinal fluid.
- **t2** contested: The Suśrutasaṃhitā describes preparing a body for study by leaving it in water for several days and then removing its layers one by one; specialist scholarship treats it as a layered text whose composition may have begun in the last centuries BCE, not as a 6th-century BCE work.
- **t3** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): In the Huangdi Neijing the vessels carry qi and only sometimes blood, and specialist translators read it as describing neither blood circulation nor a pumping heart.
- **t4** established · secondary-summary (key scholarship read only via summaries/abstracts): Ibn al-Haytham wrote the Book of Optics between about 1011 and 1021 and argued for intromission, in which light from every point of an object enters the eye.
- **t5** established: In the thirteenth century Ibn al-Nafis rejected the septal pores and described blood passing from the right ventricle through the lungs to the left; in Europe the route was described in the 1550s.
