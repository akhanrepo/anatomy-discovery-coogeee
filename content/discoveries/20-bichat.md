Xavier Bichat died at 30, having changed the level at which we look at the body.

Before him, anatomy and medicine thought mainly in organs: the liver, the lung, the heart. Bichat noticed that very different organs share the same kinds of material. The lining of the chest and of the abdomen behave alike; so do membranes in places far apart. In his *Traité des membranes* of 1800 he proposed that the body is built from a limited set of tissues, combined in different ways to make organs, and he distinguished twenty-one of them.[^c1]

He did it without a microscope. Bichat sorted tissues by what he could see and feel: texture, elasticity, transparency, how they reacted to heat or injury.[^c2] He never saw a cell.

The idea moved disease, too, from the organ to the tissue, and it prepared the ground for cell theory a generation later. On the body, skin, muscle and the meninges ink in as outlines here: newly described as kinds of tissue.
