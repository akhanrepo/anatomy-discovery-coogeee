Some of the most practical anatomy of the ancient world is found in a Sanskrit surgical text, the *Suśrutasaṃhitā*. It says that a surgeon who wants to know the body must see it, and it explains how.

The instructions are ingenious. A suitable body is placed in water and left for several days. Once it has softened, the outer skin is gently rubbed or scraped away, then the next layer, and the next, so that skin, flesh and the structures beneath can be seen in order.[^c1] The secondary accounts don't agree on all the details. Some describe a cage in running water and seven days; others describe grass covering and fewer days. We follow the text, and mark the details as contested.

The date needs equal care. Popular websites often place Suśruta in the sixth century BCE. Specialist scholarship sees a text built up in layers, whose composition may have begun in the last centuries BCE and was completed later.[^c2] That is still remarkably early for a written method of dissection.

On the body, the skin and muscles ink in as outlines here: carefully described, layer by layer.
