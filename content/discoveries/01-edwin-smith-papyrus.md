The scroll is a surgeon's manual, not a book of spells. Someone copied it around 1600 BCE from texts that were already old,[^c1] and it works through injuries from the head downwards: a wound, an examination, a diagnosis, a decision.

In those head cases we meet the brain in writing for the first time. The author describes what lies under a broken skull: the folded surface of the brain, a membrane over it, and fluid. These are the oldest known written accounts of the meninges and the cerebrospinal fluid, and they contain the oldest known word for "brain".[^c2]

What makes the document feel modern is its honesty. Each case ends with a verdict. Some injuries are "an ailment I will treat". Others are judged beyond help.[^c3] The surgeon noticed that a blow to the head could affect the limbs, but nobody yet knew why.

So the brain enters history as something seen in an injury, before anyone understood what it does. On this page it starts as an outline: described, not yet understood.
