Around 1512, Leonardo da Vinci turned his attention to the ox heart, and in particular to the three little cusps of the aortic valve. Why do they close so neatly after each beat?

His answer came from a lifetime of watching water. In the bulging pockets just above the valve, the sinuses, he drew blood swirling into eddies. Those whirlpools, he reasoned, press the cusps shut as the flow slows.[^c1] To test it he described building a glass model of the aortic root, and filling it with water and grass seeds so the swirls would show.

He was right. Twentieth-century flow studies, and later MRI, showed vortices in the sinuses behind the cusps doing much what he had drawn.[^c2] Whether he actually built the glass model, or only planned it, is disputed.[^c3]

And here is the twist. Leonardo never published his anatomy. The notebooks were scattered and the insight sat unread, so it shaped nothing for centuries. On this site the fog records when something was first *understood* by anyone, so the valve inks in here, but with an asterisk: nobody else knew.
