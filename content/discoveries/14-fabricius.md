Tie a cord around your upper arm and look at your forearm: small knots appear along the veins. Those are valves, and in 1603 the Paduan anatomist Hieronymus Fabricius published the first detailed study of them, *De venarum ostiolis*, *On the Little Doors of the Veins*.[^c1] Others had glimpsed them, but nobody had described them so well.

Then he explained them, and got it backwards. In Galen's system, blood flows *outwards* through the veins from the liver to nourish the body. Fabricius reasoned that the valves act as brakes, slowing that outward flow so the blood would not pool in the hands and feet.[^c2]

The anatomy was right; the physiology was upside down. One of the students in Padua was a young Englishman called William Harvey. He would look at the same little doors and see that they open only one way, *towards* the heart.[^c3]

The valves ink in here as outlines. The red line, "valves slow the outward flow", is drawn alongside them, waiting for 1628.
