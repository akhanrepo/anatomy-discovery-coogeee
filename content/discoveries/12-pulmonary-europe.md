Three hundred years after Ibn al-Nafis, the lung circuit appeared in Europe, three times in six years, and nobody seems to have been copying anybody.

The first is the strangest. In 1553 the Spanish theologian Michael Servetus described blood passing from the heart through the lungs to be refreshed by air. He did it in a book about the Holy Spirit, *Christianismi restitutio*, published anonymously.[^c1] Servetus was burned in Geneva that year, and nearly every copy of his book went with him; three survive.

Anatomy took a more respectable route. In 1556 Juan Valverde de Hamusco described the pulmonary circulation in his anatomy book and gave the credit to his teacher, Realdo Colombo. Colombo's own *De re anatomica* came out in 1559, the year he died.[^c2] With Servetus suppressed and Ibn al-Nafis unprinted, Colombo seems to have got there on his own.[^c3]

Who was "first"? It depends on whether you count a theology book, a student's textbook or the teacher's own. That's why the plate is marked *contested*, and why the septal pores now fade in European teaching too.
