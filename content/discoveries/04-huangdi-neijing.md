You may have read that Chinese physicians described the circulation of the blood two thousand years before William Harvey. It is one of the most repeated "firsts" in popular history. This plate is here to take it apart carefully, because the truth is more interesting.

The claim is old. In 1685 the Dutch scholar Isaac Vossius, keen to show that Chinese learning outshone Europe's, wrote that the Chinese had known of the circulation for thousands of years, pointing to texts such as the *Huangdi Neijing*.[^c1] In the twentieth century, Joseph Needham's translations seemed to support a circulation of vital energy driven by the heart.

Specialist translators disagree. Paul Unschuld reads the same passages as describing neither a circulation of blood nor a heart that pumps.[^c2] In the classic, the vessels carry *qi*, and only sometimes blood.[^c3] It is a sophisticated model of flow and correspondence, and it deserves to be understood in its own terms rather than graded against Harvey.
