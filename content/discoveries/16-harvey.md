William Harvey's little book of 1628, *On the Motion of the Heart and Blood in Animals*, printed in Frankfurt,[^c1] did not discover a new organ. It changed what the old ones were for.

Galen's system had blood made continuously in the liver from food, sent outwards through the veins, and used up by the body. Harvey asked a bookkeeper's question: how much? Estimate how much blood the heart pushes out with each beat, multiply by the beats in an hour, and the total is far more than the whole body contains, and far more than anyone eats. The blood can't be made and used up at that rate. It must go round.[^c2]

He backed the sums with experiments on many animals, and with one you can do yourself. Press on a vein in the forearm and try to push the blood away from the heart: the valves stop you. The little doors that Fabricius thought were brakes allow flow only towards the heart.[^c3]

Harvey could not see how blood got from arteries to veins. That gap would be filled in 1661. But on this plate the heart and vessels ink in fully, and three red beliefs are scraped away.
