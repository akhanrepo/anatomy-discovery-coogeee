Popular histories say Antonie van Leeuwenhoek discovered sperm. Leeuwenhoek himself said otherwise.

In August 1677 a medical student named Johan Ham visited the Delft draper-turned-microscopist with a sample of semen. Under the microscope he had seen tiny moving creatures, which he thought were a sign of decay.[^c1] Leeuwenhoek looked through his own lenses, tiny single beads of glass that magnified far better than the compound microscopes of the day, and saw them too. He thought they were no accident of decay but a normal part of the fluid.

That November he wrote to the Royal Society in London describing the "animalcules", and recorded that Ham had seen them first. The original letter is lost, but a Latin translation was printed in the *Philosophical Transactions*.[^c2]

What the cells actually *do* in conception would take much longer to work out. So the testes ink in here as an outline: seen at a new scale, not yet understood. Find them on the male figure.
